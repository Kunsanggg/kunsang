Does Your Writing Hand Perform Better Than Your Other Hand?

What We Did

Our class completed a writing activity using our writing hands and other hands. We compared the class results and looked at the Yes and No groups. We used three statistical tests to compare the results.

My Results

Test Mean 1 Mean 2 Difference p-value
One-sample 6.333 8.000 -1.667 0.001053
Independent-samples 6.400 6.250 0.150 0.8481
Paired-samples 6.333 2.556 3.778 0.00000647

The Three Write-Ups

  1. One-sample: The writing-hand average was 1.667 lower than the benchmark. The p-value was below 0.05, so the result was significant.
  2. Independent-samples: The Yes group scored 0.150 higher than the No group. The p-value was above 0.05, so the result was not significant.
  3. Paired-samples: The writing hand scored 3.778 higher than the other hand. The p-value was below 0.05, so the result was significant.

In Plain Words

The writing hand had a higher average than the other hand. The Yes and No groups had almost the same results. These results show differences but do not prove what caused them.

What I Would Do Differently

I would include more students and use more classrooms. I would also change the order of the writing activity so practice and tiredness would not affect the results as much.
