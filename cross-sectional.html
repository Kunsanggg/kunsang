Cross-Sectional Studies

 

The researchers said that "a generation is becoming more anxious as they grow older." Their study found that the anxiety of adults from 18-25 rose from 8.1% in 2014 to 14.7% in 2023.

 

The study used 27,000 new adults each year from 2014 to 2023. They asked each person about their anxiety during the past 30 days. They did not ask the same people every year.

 

This is where the claim has issues. The study shows that more young adults reported anxiety in 2023 than in 2014. But it does not have any evidence that the same adults that they surveyed in 2014 became more anxious in the coming years. Later, in 2019, they changed the survey method from in-person to phone and web surveys.

 

This shows an important limit of cross-sectional data. It shows how a group changes as time passes, but it cannot show how the same people change over time.

 

For me a better conclusion would be: "More adults reported anxiety in 2023 than in 2014. However, there is no evidence that shows that the same people became more anxious as they got older."

 

Kuisang Lama
PH 320 · Applied Biostatistics in Public Health
