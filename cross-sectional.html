Cross-Sectional Studies

 

The Metropolitan Wellness Assessment found out that regular physical exercise reduces the burden of depression in adults, and counties should invest in exercise programming as a mental health intervention. The study conducted found that 11% of the adults who exercised four or more days a week reported frequent depressive symptoms, in contrast to 27% of adults who exercised once a week or less.

 

The study was conducted between March and May 2021 with a survey of 28 questions, and 4,200 adults completed this survey from 6,000 households in one metropolitan county. The questions were about how many days they exercised in the past week and how often they felt depressed in the past week.

 

The study tried to show the connection between exercise and depression. It's not very clear, and they don't have any actual evidence that says exercise regularly can reduce depression. They only asked two questions and tried to link them. I mean, there may be other factors that can cause depression.

 

This can be the main limitation of cross-sectional studies. It cannot say which came first and whether one is the cause of another.

 

A fair conclusion could be: Adults who exercise more often were less likely to report depressive symptoms, but this study cannot show that exercise caused the difference.

 

Kuisang Lama
PH 320 · Applied Biostatistics in Public Health
